Lesson 2 (already published, keep consistent): "Facts First: Fact, Assumption, Inference, Conclusion".
Bottom line: most mistakes come from a "fact" that was never a fact; sort every statement into fact,
assumption, inference, conclusion. Framework: fact = provable with document/record/witness; assumption =
believed but unchecked; inference = reasoning step from facts; conclusion inherits the weakest box.
Every fact carries a source and date; every assumption an owner and a check.
Worked example (hypothetical): lot for 400,000; "seller owns it outright" (assumption until title search),
"zoning allows four units" (assumption until written confirmation), "closes in 30 days" (inference).
If zoning allows only two units at 150,000 per unit, value falls from 600,000 to 300,000 — a 300,000
assumption tested by a one-page letter. Sign-off: real estate counsel (title); planning professional (zoning).
Beginner mistake: treating a confident tone as proof; writing assumptions in the same style as facts.
Checklist: list statements; label each; source+date for facts; owner+deadline for assumptions; check the
assumption that moves the most money first; write the conclusion as "yes, if...".
Self-test: "My company can lend me 50,000. My accountant said it was fine last year, and the shareholders
will not mind." Model answer: remembered statement = assumption until in writing for this loan;
shareholders = prediction, check is a signed resolution; the loan = conclusion resting on both.
Apply today: two-column page, know (source, date) vs assuming (name, check).

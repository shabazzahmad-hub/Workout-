# Elite Developer — writer's brief (read in full before writing)

You are writing finished lessons for "Elite Developer", a 131-week course for Mr Shabazz
(address him as "Mr Shabazz"). The faculty is the 592 Marathon specialist bench; Kema is chief of
staff. The full week-by-week syllabus is in CURRICULUM.md in this folder. Each WEEK has a subject and a
lead teacher. Each week has 7 lessons (Day 1 to Day 7). Lesson number n = (week-1)*7 + day.

The reader may open ANY lesson on ANY day, out of order, and will either read it or press Listen,
which reads the lesson aloud with the phone's voice. So every lesson must stand on its own, read well,
AND sound right when read aloud.

## Who he is
Technically capable: C.E.T., construction project management training, military and power-grid
operations background (shift worker, reliability coordinator). Assume intelligence, not prior
knowledge. Zero flattery. He is building a career as a real estate developer and wealth builder.

## Scope rules (non-negotiable)
- Standalone teaching. NOT project work. Never mention Parcel 786, Great Diamond, 592 Marathon project
  figures, his entities, his family or his finances. Never mention a six-storey program, an anchor-tenant
  master lease, CNQC, or Marathon-Enterprise/MEI. Hypothetical round numbers only, labelled hypothetical.
- Law and tax: teach concepts and how to think; no jurisdictional conclusions. Never state a rate,
  threshold, deadline or section number as fact; write "verify that against the primary source before
  you rely on it."
- Wealth: conceptual only; no products, funds or securities recommended; "a licensed adviser is
  required before you act."
- Trust and estate examples are hypothetical, never his family.
- Securities lessons never script real investor outreach.
- Building topics: American standards (IBC, ACI, AISC, ASCE, NFPA, ASHRAE, ASTM, ASME, AWS, NEC, OSHA).
  Chinese GB codes are never a design basis. Never invent a section number; flag unverified
  provisions: "verify against the current adopted edition."
- Ratios, lending terms and contract percentages are commercial practice, not standards; say so.
- Psychology/physiology: established research or labelled practitioner habit. No ego depletion or power
  posing as fact. Correct the "93 percent non-verbal" myth if raised. Breathing drills only when parked,
  never while driving. "Check that with your doctor."
- Name the licensed professional a real decision needs: "Professional sign-off required: <discipline>."
- Failure cases: real ones only when you are confident of facts and dates; otherwise label
  "This is a composite example." Do not invent statistics, quotes or company figures. When unsure of a
  number, describe it without the number.
- Standard of thinking: world-class, specific, practical. Depth over breadth. Teach at most three new
  terms per lesson.

## Lesson structure (body HTML), in order
1. <p class="lede"> one or two sentences: the bottom line of the lesson.
2. <h3>Recall</h3> three quick questions from earlier subjects (about 4, 14 and 56 lessons back, using
   CURRICULUM.md; use what exists), each as <p><strong>Q.</strong> ...</p><p><strong>A.</strong> ...</p>.
   Skip this section for Week 1 Days 1-3.
3. <h3>Why this matters</h3> to a developer and wealth builder who runs his own projects.
4. Two to four <h3> teaching sections: plain English, then the professional framing, then an applied
   example. At least two worked examples in the lesson. Once, mid-lesson, add
   <p class="say">Say that out loud now.</p>
5. <h3>The biggest risk</h3> and the control that stops it.
6. <h3>The beginner's mistake</h3>
7. (The checklist goes in the "checklist" field, NOT the body.)
8. <h3>Callback</h3> one paragraph linking to an idea from at least three lessons back.
9. <h3>Recap</h3> a short paragraph, then one line naming the next lesson's teacher and subject.
Allowed tags only: h3, p, ol, ul, li, strong, em, blockquote, table, tr, th, td. No markdown, no
emoji, no inline styles, no links. The only classes: p.lede, p.say.
Written for the eye AND the ear: plain sentences, define every term the first time, spell out
abbreviations the first time they appear ("net operating income (NOI)"). Numbers as digits are fine.
LENGTH: body 1,300 to 1,700 words. Speak, terms and hostile are extra.

## JSON format — one file per week
Write the file lessons/wNNN.json (NNN = week number padded to 3 digits, e.g. w014.json) containing a JSON
array of exactly 7 objects, Day 1 to Day 7:
{
 "n": <lesson number>, "w": <week>, "d": <day>,
 "angle": "First Principles" | "Failure Forensics" | "The Money" | "The Rules" | "The Numbers" |
          "The Room" | "Quiz & Recap" | "Checkpoint",
 "who": "<teacher name>", "role": "<teacher role>",
 "title": "<lesson title, plain text, no HTML>",
 "summary": "<two or three plain sentences>",
 "body": "<the lesson HTML as one string>",
 "checklist": ["<six to eight plain-text items>"],
 "head": "Field checklist" (or "The six questions" on D7, "Rules I will not break" on CP D7),
 "speak": "<the model sixty-second version, about 150 words, plain text>",
 "terms": ["Term — one-sentence definition", "...", "..."],
 "hostile": {"q": "<the hard question a lender, investor, auditor or contractor would ask>",
             "a": "<the model answer>"},
 "rule": "Rule <week>: ..."   (ONLY on Day 2)
}
Angle for checkpoint weeks: D1-D6 use their normal angle; D7 uses "Checkpoint". Non-CP D7 uses
"Quiz & Recap". Plain text fields contain no HTML. Use real UTF-8 characters (em dash, apostrophes).
The file must be valid JSON: write it with a small Python script (json.dump with ensure_ascii=False,
indent=1) rather than by hand, and then validate it with: python3 -c "import json;d=json.load(open('FILE'));assert len(d)==7"

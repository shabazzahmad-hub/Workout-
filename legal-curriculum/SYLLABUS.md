# Think Like a Lawyer — 56-day course

Prepared for: Mr Ahmad Shabazz (592 Marathon)
Status: v01_DRAFT, education only. Not legal, tax or financial advice.
Cadence: one lesson a day, about 10 minutes (about 800 words), 7 days a week.
Delivery: a private Claude page per lesson, with a Listen button, plus a push notification.
Order: foundations first, then structure, then tax, then money, then land, then estate.

## Standing rules for every lesson

1. Teach in this order: plain English, then the professional framework, then one worked example,
   then one beginner mistake, then a checklist. End with one self-test question and one task to apply today.
2. Never state a 592 Marathon project figure (cost, rent, revenue, raise size, storeys, DSCR).
   Use round hypothetical numbers and label them "hypothetical".
3. Never name any retired or void fact from the project kill list. The former anchor tenant is
   never named. Marathon-Enterprise Inc. is never mentioned. The six-storey program is never mentioned.
4. Jurisdictional law (Canada, Alberta, Guyana) is taught as concepts. Any statute, rate, treaty
   article or case named must carry a citation to a primary source the lesson author verified
   that day, or be tagged "[unverified — confirm independently]".
5. Where a licensed professional must sign off before acting, write
   "Professional Sign-Off Required: [discipline]".
6. Plain words. Short sentences. Define every term the first time. No idioms.
7. The reader is a developer, not a law student. Every lesson ends with how he would use it.

## The 14 topics and 56 lessons

### Topic 1 — Thinking like a lawyer (lessons 1–4)
1. The question behind the question: IRAC and reframing
2. Facts first: fact, assumption, inference, conclusion
3. Reading a rule: elements, exceptions, burdens
4. Risk thinking: worst case, probability, reversibility, one-way doors

### Topic 2 — Legal document structuring (5–8)
5. Anatomy of a contract: parties, recitals, definitions, operative clauses, boilerplate
6. Definitions and precision: how one word moves money
7. Conditions precedent, covenants, representations, warranties
8. Remedies, termination, dispute resolution, governing law

### Topic 3 — AI as a legal tool (9–12)
9. What AI does better than most lawyers, and what it cannot do
10. Verify before asserting: primary sources and hallucination traps
11. Drafting with AI: rubric, red team, defect list
12. The brief to counsel: arrive prepared, cut fees

### Topic 4 — Corporate law (13–16)
13. The corporation as a separate person: limited liability and its limits
14. Shares, classes and rights: voting, dividends, redemption
15. Directors, officers, shareholders: duties and the minute book
16. Shareholder agreements: control, exits, deadlock, death

### Topic 5 — Holdco and Opco structuring (17–20)
17. Why two companies: the creditor wall
18. Moving value upstream: intercorporate dividends (concepts)
19. Lending between related companies and documenting it
20. Operating discipline: separate books, separate bank, no commingling

### Topic 6 — Trust structuring (21–24)
21. What a trust is: settlor, trustee, beneficiary, trust property
22. Discretionary family trusts: what discretion buys and costs
23. Trustee duties and the trust deed: powers, protectors, replacement
24. Trusts and tax: attribution and the deemed disposition clock (concepts)

### Topic 7 — Cross-border business structuring (25–28)
25. Where a company lives: incorporation, residence, management and control
26. Stacking entities across borders: what each layer buys
27. Local content, beneficial ownership and look-through rules
28. Repatriating money: dividends, loans, fees and leakage

### Topic 8 — CRA tax advisor (29–32)
29. How the CRA thinks: self-assessment, audit triggers, documentation
30. Corporate tax basics: active income, investment income, integration (concepts)
31. Personal vs corporate: salary, dividends, shareholder loans and benefits
32. Compliance calendar: filings, elections and the cost of being late

### Topic 9 — Cross-border tax strategist (33–36)
33. Residence and source: who taxes what
34. Tax treaties: what they do, tie-breakers, withholding
35. Foreign affiliates: exempt and taxable surplus (concepts, sign-off required)
36. Anti-avoidance: GAAR thinking and substance over form

### Topic 10 — Financial architect (37–40)
37. The capital stack: equity, debt, mezzanine, who is paid first
38. Reading a lender: DSCR, LTV, covenants, bankability
39. Pro forma discipline: sources and uses, sensitivities, the killing number
40. Gates and sequencing: what must be true before you ask for money

### Topic 11 — Wealth architect (41–44)
41. Capital preservation first: margin of safety and priorities
42. Asset location vs asset allocation
43. Liquidity, concentration and the developer's trap
44. Generational design: control today, transfer tomorrow

### Topic 12 — Land conveyance (45–48)
45. Title: what you own and how it is proved
46. The conveyance process: agreement, searches, transfer, registration
47. Encumbrances: mortgages, liens, easements, lender consents
48. Transferring land into a company: triggers, costs, timing

### Topic 13 — Estate planning (49–52)
49. What estate planning is: control, taxes, who decides when you cannot
50. Powers of attorney and personal directives
51. The deemed disposition at death and how structures soften it (concepts)
52. Beneficiary designations, joint ownership and probate traps

### Topic 14 — Will planning (53–56)
53. What a valid will needs and what makes one fail
54. Executors, guardians, specific vs residual gifts
55. Wills and trusts together: testamentary and spousal trusts, minors
56. The review cycle: updates, cross-border wills, final checklist
